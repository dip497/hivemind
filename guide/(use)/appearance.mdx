Change the look when you want other colours, a calmer background, or more contrast. Every change
applies at once and is saved for you.

## Themes

A theme is a whole look that a package brings: colours, an accent, and often terminal colours,
corners, fonts and a wallpaper. Install a package that holds one from **Settings → Plugins**, on
the **HiveHub** tab, or with `hive plugins install @owner/package`. A package of only themes,
views and widgets arrives switched on.

Its themes then show under **Settings → Appearance → Themes**, each with five colours (its
background, text, accent, done and failed, as a preset shows), its package and version, and **Apply**. Press **Apply** and the theme is written over your look:

- What the theme leaves out stays yours. Glass is always yours.
- Its fonts and wallpaper are copied beside your settings, so the look keeps working without the
  package.
- A theme's wallpaper shows even with glass off.
- Your look before it is kept. **Revert to previous look** puts back what the theme set, and
  nothing else: corners, fonts or a wallpaper the theme left out and you changed since stay.

```bash
hive theme list                   # presets, and themes as @owner/package/theme
hive theme use @owner/kit/harbour
hive theme revert                 # back to the look before it
```

When a newer version of the package is installed, the row says so:
"Harbour 1.2 is installed; you have 1.1 applied." Press **Apply 1.2**, or run `hive theme use`
again. **Revert to previous look** still goes back to the look before the theme.

Removing the package keeps the look. The row then reads "Using Harbour 1.1 (its package was
removed)", and **Revert to previous look** still works. Picking a **Preset** leaves the theme:
Revert goes, and so does a theme's own accent colour.

A guest in your shared workspace sees the theme's colours and accent, but not its fonts or its
picture wallpaper. The phone page keeps its own colours.

### An accent of your own

Under **Accent**, the last circle opens a colour picker: pick any colour. The accent marks the
selected tile, sliders, links and the text cursor. A colour of your own, or a theme's, also fills
the main button, a switch that is on and the focus ring. If it is hard to see on your
background or on the panels, the row says so with its contrast. Picking a built-in accent or a **Preset** drops
your own colour.

To set it from a terminal, with a second colour for the accent's lighter uses:

```bash
hive config set appearanceExtra.accent '{"brand":"#c08552","accent":"#d9a273"}'
```

To make a theme of your own, see [Packages](/packages#themes).

## Words on this page

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Glass** | Frosted, see-through panels over the background. |
| **Overlays** | Images or videos placed over the whole workspace, such as a transparent animation. |
| **Tools in scene views** | How terminals and editors look inside a view you installed: **Use my theme** draws them as on the canvas; **Opaque** gives them a solid background. |

The background, glass and overlays show under the **Canvas** and **Windows** views. A view you
installed paints its own background.

## Share or keep your theme

Under **Advanced**, **Theme file** has **Copy JSON** and **Import…**. **Terminal colours** sets
the 16 terminal colours, the background, text, cursor and selection.

```bash
hive theme export my-theme.json
hive theme import my-theme.json
```

The file holds no media: your photo, video and overlays stay on this computer, and the export says
what it left out. Importing a file keeps the ones you have. If the theme used a photo or video,
pick your own under **Background**.

**Import…** and `hive theme import` also take a whole settings.json, and read only the look from
any file. What a file leaves out, such as light or dark, stays as you have it. A file with none of
the look in it, such as a package.json, is refused and changes nothing. The file carries your own
accent colour; importing one over a theme leaves the theme, so **Revert to previous look** does not undo it. To
keep a copy of your look with its photo, video and overlays, save `hive config get > my-look.json`:
importing that file puts them back.

A guest who joins your workspace sees your colours and accent, your own accent colour too, but not your photo, video, overlays or fonts.

## Check it worked

- The window shows the theme you picked.
- `hive config get appearance.preset` prints its name.

## If it didn't

| You see | Do this |
|---|---|
| `hive config set` refuses a value | The value must be JSON: text in double quotes inside single quotes (`'"ice"'`). Its range is in the [Settings reference](/settings#appearance). |
| `hive config set` says this version has no such setting | Check the path. The message lists the settings in that group, and the one you may mean (`appearance.glass.blur` for `appearance.blur`). |
| The text is hard to read over a photo | Lower **Media brightness**, or raise **Panel opacity**. |
| An agent keeps its dark colours after you switch to a light terminal | An agent that asks to hear of the change (Claude Code does) switches at once. Others, such as Codex and Gemini CLI, read the background when they start: restart them in that tile. |
| Something else | [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting) |
